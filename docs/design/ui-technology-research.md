# UI technology research

Researched on 2026-11-07 for the UI session in `docs/agents/handoff.md`. Versions and dates are as of that day, so check them again before relying on them later. Nothing here was installed, built or run.

**Result:** the choice comes down to one thing, which is who owns the renderer when 3D arrives. Bevy with egui panels (`bevy_egui`) owns it from day one, at the price of upgrade churn and heavier builds. eframe with a custom wgpu canvas, the way Rerun is built, is lighter now and leaves the 3D renderer to be written or adopted later. Tauri with three.js gets the best-looking forms and an easy 3D renderer, but puts a boundary and a second language in a project whose first pillar is performance. I lean to Bevy with `bevy_egui`, as a suggestion. See "Shortlist".

**How to read this:** a statement next to a link is what that source says. **Inference** marks my own reasoning, and **calculated** marks my own arithmetic. Neither has been measured.

## What the load means for any option

- **Calculated:** 1.9M occurrences per second is about 31,700 per frame at 60 frames per second. An `Occurrence` should be 32 bytes in memory, from reading its fields in `src/observation.rs` (not measured). That makes about 61 MB/s, or 1 MB per frame. As JSON at about 80 bytes each, it is about 150 MB/s, before parsing.
- **Inference: occurrences needn't cross a boundary at all.** Only a frame's worth of picture has to be drawn. At peak speed MU markers are off, as agreed, so that is one state colour per object: 6 KB per frame. Zoomed in and slow, with 5,000 markers at 12 bytes each, it is about 66 KB per frame, or 4 MB/s. So the picture logic can stay in Rust in every option, which softens the handoff's point that a UI in another language has to port it.
- **Toolchain:** Rust 1.91.1 is too old for most of this. Latest stable is [1.99.1, 2026-10-16](https://github.com/rust-lang/rust/releases/tag/1.99.1). Minimums from crates.io: [Bevy 0.20.1, 1.97.1](https://crates.io/crates/bevy/0.20.1), [egui and eframe 0.37, 1.95](https://crates.io/crates/egui/0.37.0), [`bevy_egui` 0.43.1, 1.95](https://crates.io/crates/bevy_egui/0.43.1), [Slint 1.18.1, 1.92](https://crates.io/crates/slint/1.18.1), [Tauri 2.12.5, 1.90](https://crates.io/crates/tauri/2.12.5), [iced 0.14, 1.88](https://crates.io/crates/iced/0.14.0). **Inference:** `rustup update` may move the benchmark, so re-run the baseline before comparing anything to it.
- **Same in every option (inference):** the UI is a separate crate and sends commands only, so headless runs pay nothing and results don't change.

## 1. Bevy, with `bevy_egui` for panels

[0.20.1, 2026-10-28](https://crates.io/crates/bevy/0.20.1). MIT or Apache-2.0.

- **2D to 3D:** [0.19 said](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-19/) 2D had started to lag 3D in speed and features and that the internals would be unified. [0.20 replaced](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-20/) the sprite backend with one that reuses the 3D infrastructure, though [`Text2d` is still on the old one](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-19-to-0-20/).
  - **Inference, what survives:** entities and systems for objects and MUs, state colours, picking, every egui panel, and the renderer itself. What changes is sprites to meshes, the camera, and labels.
  - **Inference, the cheapest route:** draw the 2D schematic from the start as flat meshes in the 3D pipeline under an orthographic camera. The move then swaps meshes and camera. Lights, shadows and glTF loading [come with the engine](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-19/). A prototype should check this.
- **Building models:** Bevy's own UI is young. [Feathers](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/latest/crates/bevy_feathers/src/lib.rs) is a widget set for editors and inspectors, still growing, and the [official editor hasn't landed](https://bevy.org/news/bevys-sixth-birthday/), with no date. **Inference:** use egui for forms and panels, the same work as option 2. Dragging from a palette into the world is the part to try, starting from [`bevy_picking`](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/picking/index.html) and the [`dragdrop_picking`](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/latest/examples/picking/dragdrop_picking.rs) example.
- **`bevy_egui`:** [0.43.1 for Bevy 0.20](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vladbat00/bevy_egui/main/README.md). It followed the last three Bevy releases within a day ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/bevy_egui/versions)). It [requires egui `^0.36`](https://crates.io/crates/bevy_egui/0.43.1/dependencies), one behind the latest. Its examples include [a side panel beside a resized camera viewport](https://github.com/vladbat00/bevy_egui/blob/main/examples/side_panel.rs) and [absorbing input](https://github.com/vladbat00/bevy_egui/blob/main/examples/absorb_input.rs).
- **Animation and scale:** published figures are all far above 6,000, but on a different load.
  - [`many_cubes`, 1.6M cube entities with 116,000 in view](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-19/): 18.77 ms per frame on 0.19, on a laptop with a mobile RTX 4090.
  - [`bevy_city`, 55,000 rendered entities](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-19/): 11.8 ms static and 16.2 ms moving, hardware not named.
  - [`many_sprites`](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/latest/examples/stress_tests/many_sprites.rs) is 102,400 sprites (calculated), with no published time. Its comment warns that colour tints split batches.
  - [`MeshTag`](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/mesh/struct.MeshTag.html) gives each instance an index into a storage buffer, as in the colour example [`storage_buffer`](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/latest/examples/shader/storage_buffer.rs). **Inference:** that suits a state colour per object.
- **Render on change:** [`WinitSettings::desktop_app`](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/winit/struct.WinitSettings.html) updates on input or a redraw request, and a redraw can be requested from outside through `EventLoopProxy`. **Inference:** continuous while Advancing, reactive while Halted.
- **In process.** The [official example](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/latest/examples/async_tasks/external_source_external_thread.rs) runs an outside thread and drains a channel with `try_iter` in a system. It uses crossbeam, because std's `Receiver` isn't `Sync` and Bevy resources must be. **Inference:** `LiveSession` holds a std `Receiver`, so wrap it in a `Mutex` or keep it as a non-send resource. Draining 31,700 occurrences a frame is unmeasured in every in-process option.
- **Maturity and churn:** the highest of the options. Releases [0.17 on 2025-09-30, 0.18 on 2026-01-13, 0.19 on 2026-06-19, 0.20 on 2026-10-08](https://bevy.org/news/), so one every 3.5 to 5 months. The migration guides have about [103 sections for 0.19](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-18-to-0-19/) and [75 for 0.20](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-19-to-0-20/). The [sixth-birthday post](https://bevy.org/news/bevys-sixth-birthday/) names no 1.0 and says the foundation is underfunded. **Inference:** nothing forces an upgrade, so pin a version and take one when it's worth it. Custom render code is what churn hurts most.
- **Install and ship:** [a current stable Rust, as Bevy's stated minimum is the latest stable, plus the Visual Studio C++ build tools](https://bevy.org/learn/quick-start/getting-started/setup/). The page gives no clean-build figure, and recommends `dynamic_linking`, a faster linker and optimised dependencies while developing, with dynamic linking not for shipping. **Inference:** a download is one exe plus any assets. The owner has to learn ECS.

## 2. egui and eframe, with a custom wgpu canvas

[egui 0.37.0, 2026-10-15](https://crates.io/crates/egui/0.37.0). MIT or Apache-2.0. [eframe uses wgpu by default](https://docs.rs/eframe/latest/eframe/).

- **Rerun is the real example, and shows the shape works.** [Its architecture document](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rerun-io/rerun/main/ARCHITECTURE.md) describes a viewer written entirely in egui, drawing through `re_renderer`, its own layer over wgpu. [The 2D view](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/views/re_view_spatial/src/ui_2d.rs) and [the 3D view](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/views/re_view_spatial/src/ui_3d.rs) both build a `ViewBuilder` and hand it to egui with `new_renderer_callback`. [That callback](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/viewer_support/re_viewer_context/src/gpu_bridge/re_renderer_callback.rs) renders the view in `prepare` and composites it in `paint`. [Rerun's workspace](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rerun-io/rerun/main/Cargo.toml), at 0.43.0-alpha on `main` ([0.42.0 released on 2026-11-04](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/releases/tag/0.42.0)), is on egui 0.37 and wgpu 30 with no active patches to either, so it runs on released egui.
- **2D to 3D. Inference:** egui panels, forms, docking and the picture logic all survive, because Rerun's panels don't care which view is inside them. The canvas is the problem: a 2D instanced pipeline doesn't become 3D by itself. Meshes, depth, lights, shadows, materials, asset loading and ray-cast picking have to be written or adopted, which is section 3. `re_renderer` is [132 Rust and shader files, 1.27 MB of source](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/tree/main/crates/ui_tools/re_renderer) (counted from the GitHub tree), and its [lighting is still two fixed lights and ambient](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/ui_tools/re_renderer/shader/utils/lighting.wgsl), so "good-looking 3D" is further still.
- **Building models:** the strongest of the Rust options, with the same panels as option 1. Ready-made parts include [`Scene` for pan and zoom](https://docs.rs/egui/latest/egui/containers/struct.Scene.html), [`dnd_drag_source` and `dnd_drop_zone`](https://github.com/emilk/egui/blob/main/crates/egui/src/drag_and_drop.rs) and the ordinary form widgets. **Inference:** forms and panels are little work, a few widget calls each. Selecting, moving and connecting objects on the canvas is your own code, in every Rust option.
  - **Docking:** [`egui_dock` 0.21.1, 2026-08-06, on egui `^0.36`](https://crates.io/crates/egui_dock/0.21.1), its repository [moved to `anhosh/egui_dock`](https://github.com/anhosh/egui_dock). [`egui_tiles` 0.18.0, 2026-10-19](https://crates.io/crates/egui_tiles/0.18.0), from Rerun, already on `^0.37`.
  - **Node graphs:** [`egui-snarl` 0.12.0, 2026-08-26](https://crates.io/crates/egui-snarl/0.12.0) and [`egui_graphs` 0.32.0, 2026-08-31](https://crates.io/crates/egui_graphs/0.32.0) are maintained, both on `^0.36`. [`egui_node_graph2` 0.7.0, 2024-11-03](https://crates.io/crates/egui_node_graph2) looks stale. **Inference:** skip them, since one surface has to both edit and animate 6,000 objects, so the canvas is custom either way.
- **Animation and scale:**
  - [The README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilk/egui/main/README.md) says most cases take 1 to 2 ms per frame, and that a full layout runs every frame.
  - The painter tessellates on the CPU, [optionally in parallel](https://github.com/emilk/egui/blob/main/crates/epaint/Cargo.toml), and I found no published throughput.
  - [`CallbackTrait`](https://docs.rs/egui-wgpu/latest/egui_wgpu/trait.CallbackTrait.html) gives `prepare`, `finish_prepare` and `paint` for custom wgpu drawing. [re_renderer draws all points in one call](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/ui_tools/re_renderer/src/renderer/point_cloud.rs) and [instances meshes](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/ui_tools/re_renderer/src/renderer/mesh_renderer.rs), but [its textured rectangles take a draw call each](https://github.com/rerun-io/rerun/blob/main/crates/ui_tools/re_renderer/src/renderer/rectangles.rs).
  - **Inference:** the painter is fine for the first demo model. Plan on the callback for 6,000 objects and for gliding MUs.
- **Render on change:** [`request_repaint` and `request_repaint_after`](https://docs.rs/egui/latest/egui/struct.Context.html#method.request_repaint), and a call from another thread wakes the UI on eframe.
- **In process,** with the same unmeasured drain as above.
- **Maturity and churn:** [the README warns](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilk/egui/main/README.md) that new releases break things. Releases [0.34 on 2026-03-26, 0.35 on 2026-06-25, 0.36 on 2026-08-05, 0.37 on 2026-10-15](https://crates.io/crates/egui/versions). [The changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilk/egui/main/CHANGELOG.md) shows real breaks, such as `App::update` replaced in 0.34. **Inference:** smaller than Bevy's, though wgpu's major versions change too. Companion crates can lag: `egui_dock` is still on 0.36 three weeks after 0.37.
- **Install and ship:** only `rustup update`. **Inference:** one exe, and the fastest builds of the shortlist. The owner would have to learn wgpu and WGSL, and that grows a lot with 3D.

## 4. iced and Slint

**iced** [0.14.0, 2025-12-07](https://crates.io/crates/iced/0.14.0), MIT.

- **Maturity:** the previous release was [0.13 on 2024-09-18](https://crates.io/crates/iced/0.13.0), 15 months earlier. [The README calls it experimental](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iced-rs/iced/master/README.md). [Master](https://github.com/iced-rs/iced/blob/master/Cargo.toml) is active as 0.15.0-dev on wgpu 30, while 0.14 is on [wgpu 27](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iced-rs/iced/master/CHANGELOG.md).
- **Canvas and 3D:** [0.14 added](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iced-rs/iced/master/CHANGELOG.md) reactive rendering and an animation API. [Its shader widget](https://docs.rs/iced/latest/iced/widget/shader/index.html) hands [`prepare` and `render` the wgpu device and encoder](https://github.com/iced-rs/iced/blob/master/wgpu/src/primitive.rs), the same route as egui's callback.
- **Building models:** `pane_grid` [splits panes and drags them](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iced-rs/iced/master/CHANGELOG.md). I found no tabbed docking crate or scale figures.
- **Inference:** the same 3D problem as egui with a smaller set of parts around it and no Rerun to copy, so nothing here beats option 2.

**Slint** [1.18.1, 2026-09-21](https://crates.io/crates/slint/1.18.1).

- **Licence:** [GPLv3, a royalty-free licence or a paid one](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/slint-ui/slint/master/LICENSE.md). The [royalty-free terms](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/slint-ui/slint/master/LICENSES/LicenseRef-Slint-Royalty-free-2.0.md) allow desktop apps and exclude embedded systems. They require an `AboutSlint` screen or a "Made with Slint" badge on a public page, and an application may not expose Slint's APIs. **Inference:** workable for a free download. Check that last clause again when users can add their own code.
- **Building models:** [`DragArea` and `DropArea` arrived in 1.17, and `Path` animation in 1.18](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/slint-ui/slint/master/CHANGELOG.md). UI is written in its own `.slint` language, and I found no docking element.
- **Canvas and 3D:** the route is [`slint::wgpu_30`](https://docs.rs/slint/latest/slint/wgpu_30/index.html), a shared device with a texture or underlay. It sits behind an `unstable-wgpu-30` feature that may change in minor releases. The [Bevy example](https://github.com/slint-ui/slint/tree/master/examples/bevy/slint-hosts-bevy) is current. No published figures for thousands of elements.
- **Inference:** the canvas would be someone else's renderer in any case, so this is Bevy plus a second framework. It's the one to look at if egui's look turns out to be a problem.

## 5. Tauri 2 with a web front end

[Tauri 2.12.5, 2026-10-21](https://crates.io/crates/tauri/2.12.5), MIT or Apache-2.0. [3.0.0-alpha.6 came out on 2026-11-06](https://crates.io/crates/tauri/versions), so a major upgrade is on its way.

- **The boundary, from the source:**
  - [Events aren't designed for high throughput, and channels are the fast, ordered route](https://v2.tauri.app/develop/calling-frontend/).
  - [`Channel`](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/blob/dev/crates/tauri/src/ipc/channel.rs) sends JSON unless given `InvokeResponseBody::Raw`, which arrives as an `ArrayBuffer`. A `Channel<Vec<u8>>` becomes a JSON array of numbers, which is a trap.
  - JSON under 8,192 bytes and raw under 1,024 bytes go through `eval`, and larger payloads through a fetch. A comment there says 8 KB of JSON ran about twice as fast through `eval` on WebView2 135.
  - WebView2 itself has [shared buffers between host and renderer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/concepts/overview-features-apis), but [wry's main WebView2 file](https://github.com/tauri-apps/wry/blob/dev/src/webview2/mod.rs) doesn't mention them.
- **Published numbers:** I found none that help. [Tauri's benchmark results](https://github.com/tauri-apps/benchmark_results) have a 3 MB transfer, but as a whole-program time: on Windows CI on 2026-11-05 it was 0.574 s against 0.583 s for hello world, so it isolates nothing. One [issue reporter](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/13405) says 3 MB takes 200 ms over IPC, on an unstated route. [An open issue](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/issues/10546) reports a wry panic on a full message queue, without a reproduction.
- **What the boundary costs. Inference:** don't send occurrences. Drain and rebuild the picture in Rust on a plain thread, and send one raw message per frame, 6 to 66 KB as calculated above. That is one `eval` and one fetch a frame, which sounds fine but is unmeasured. Clicks that ask why an object is blocked become async commands.
- **2D to 3D:** three.js [0.187.0, 2026-10-30, MIT](https://www.npmjs.com/package/three) or Babylon.js [9.35.0, 2026-11-06, Apache-2.0](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@babylonjs/core). **Inference:** one renderer for both, as in Bevy, so a camera and mesh swap, and web panels survive. Crabsim's own scene code would be JavaScript or TypeScript.
- **WebGPU and WebGL:** [Chrome shipped WebGPU on Windows with Direct3D 12 in 113](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/webgpu/overview). [Microsoft says the runtime generally matches Edge Stable](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/concepts/distribution), and [Edge 153's notes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/web-platform/release-notes/153) add WebGPU features. I found no Microsoft statement that WebGPU is on by default in WebView2, and the [newest maintainer statement](https://github.com/MicrosoftEdge/WebView2Feedback/issues/1285) is from 2021. A `navigator.gpu` check on runtime 156 settles it. On dual-GPU laptops it [could land on the integrated GPU](https://github.com/MicrosoftEdge/WebView2Feedback/issues/5072), with a flag working from version 145 according to one user.
- **Building models:** the best libraries of any option. [React Flow, `@xyflow/react` 12.12.1, 2026-11-02](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@xyflow/react) is [MIT, but removing its attribution takes a Pro subscription](https://reactflow.dev/learn/troubleshooting/remove-attribution). Forms and styling are ordinary web work.
- **Animation and scale:** [three.js says `InstancedMesh`](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) reduces draw calls, and [Babylon says thin instances](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/mesh/copies/thinInstances/) carry no JavaScript cost per instance. Neither gives figures. [React Flow's performance guide](https://reactflow.dev/learn/advanced-use/performance) gives no node counts either, and [`onlyRenderVisibleElements`](https://reactflow.dev/api-reference/react-flow) adds its own overhead. **Inference:** React Flow draws DOM elements, so at 6,000 with changing colours the model canvas would be an instanced WebGL or WebGPU canvas anyway, which removes much of its appeal.
- **Install and ship:** [C++ build tools, plus Node only for a JavaScript framework](https://v2.tauri.app/start/prerequisites/), and [`cargo install tauri-cli` works without Node](https://v2.tauri.app/start/create-project/). [MSI or NSIS installers, with the WebView2 download adding 0 MB by default](https://v2.tauri.app/distribute/windows-installer/), since Windows 11 has it. [Its hello-world benchmark binary is about 2.8 MB](https://github.com/tauri-apps/benchmark_results) (`tauri-data-windows.json`). The owner has to learn TypeScript and a front-end stack.

## 6. Browser options, briefly

- **Local Rust server over WebSocket:** the same boundary and the same one-frame-per-message idea, with [binary frames as `ArrayBuffer`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebSocket/binaryType). **Inference:** compared with Tauri it adds a port and a server to run, and gains nothing for a local download. Only worth reconsidering if remote use appears.
- **Engine in WebAssembly:** on `wasm32-unknown-unknown`, [`std::thread::spawn` panics](https://doc.rust-lang.org/rustc/platform-support/wasm32-unknown-unknown.html) and [`Instant::now` panics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rust-lang/rust/main/library/std/src/sys/time/unsupported.rs). [Threads take nightly, `-Zbuild-std`, `+atomics` and special HTTP headers, the main thread can't block, and `std::thread` isn't viable](https://wasm-bindgen.github.io/wasm-bindgen/examples/raytrace.html). `LiveSession` would need rework, and it runs slower than native. **Inference:** out, given the decision on a local download.

## Comparison

| Option | Survives the move to 3D | Building models | 6,000 objects plus MU markers | Boundary | Churn and licence | Owner installs and learns |

## Shortlist

**This is a suggestion. The decision is the owner's.**

1. **Bevy with `bevy_egui`.** Forms and panels are egui, as in option 2, so the top priority costs about the same. Instanced drawing, cameras, picking and render-on-change come with the engine, and the move to 3D is the smallest of any option. The price is bigger builds, the latest Rust, an ECS to learn, and upgrades of 75 to 103 migration items if taken, though many won't touch a simple app (inference).
2. **eframe with a custom wgpu canvas.** Simplest to start and the most control, with Rerun to copy. It would be the better choice if 3D were only a maybe. When 3D comes, the owner writes a renderer or adopts one, and `re_renderer` shows the size of that job. Using Bevy at that point means rewriting the canvas.
3. **Tauri with three.js.** Choose this if the look and speed of building forms matters most. It's the only one where performance has to be defended across a boundary, and where every feature touches two languages. Its load looks fine on paper, but nobody has published a figure.

**I lean to 1, because the owner named the 3D move as the most important criterion, and this is where it costs least.** The main risk is that Bevy gets in the way of a form-heavy app, or that its churn is worse than it looks.

**Inference: the first steps limit that risk.** Whichever is chosen, the picture logic moves into the library and panels are written as functions taking `&mut egui::Ui`. Then options 1 and 2 differ only in the canvas, and the panels move between them as long as the egui version matches. A wrong choice between them costs a canvas rather than the whole UI.

## What only a throwaway prototype can settle

1. **Bevy scale on the owner's GPU:** 6,000 flat objects with state colours, 5,000 gliding markers and labels on zoom. Frame time, comparing sprites with flat meshes under an orthographic camera, since that decides how cheap the move to 3D really is.
2. **A real session behind a window:** the 2,800-object scenario at 1 simulated hour per second, draining and rebuilding every frame. The handoff says real playback on a real clock is unmeasured, and the 16 ms tick may stutter. This is needed in any option.
3. **Panels and world together:** dragging from an egui palette into the Bevy world, input going to the right layer, clicking one of 6,000 objects, and reactive rendering while Halted.
4. **Build times and exe size on this PC,** clean and incremental, for Bevy and for eframe.
5. **The same scene in eframe,** through the painter and a wgpu callback, if option 2 is still open.
6. **Tauri, only if it stays in:** a raw channel at 6 and 66 KB per frame at 60 frames per second, with jitter, garbage collection pauses, and `navigator.gpu`.
7. **Does it look trustworthy?** Show non-technical people items 1 and 5. Nothing published can answer this.
